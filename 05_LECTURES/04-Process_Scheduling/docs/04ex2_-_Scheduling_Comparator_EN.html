Scheduling assumptions

One CPU, known service times and no context-switch cost are assumed. Non-preemptive SJF minimises average waiting for a known simultaneous batch under those assumptions; it is not universally optimal with arbitrary arrivals. Example: P1 arrives at 0 with burst 10 and P2 at 1 with burst 1; work-conserving SJF starts P1 and P2 waits 9 units, whereas permitted idling until 1 changes the optimum. SRTF is preemptive and relies on known remaining service.

When should each algorithm be considered?

FCFS

Simple batch systems where arrival order matters. Long bursts ahead can delay interactive requests.

SJF / SRTF

Consider when burst lengths can be known or estimated. The optimum depends on model assumptions; continuing short arrivals can starve long jobs.

Round Robin

Interactive and time-sharing workloads. RR cycles through the ready queue; quantum choice and queue length determine the response/overhead trade-off.

Priority

When some processes need higher priority. Age waiting tasks to reduce starvation under the stated scheduling assumptions.

Trade-offs

There is no universally best algorithm. The choice depends on the system (batch or interactive), workload distribution and objectives such as throughput, response time and fairness.
