What is Starvation?

Starvation occurs when a ready process is indefinitely denied CPU service.

Common Causes:
  • SJF: short processes keep arriving
  • Priority scheduling: higher-priority processes keep arriving
  • MLFQ without boosting: demoted processes may remain at low priority
Difference from Deadlock:

A starved process is eligible to run but is repeatedly denied service. Deadlocked processes cannot progress because their resource dependencies cannot be resolved.

Solutions for Starvation

1. Ageing

Gradually increase the priority of waiting processes. Eventual service also requires finite load and fair tie handling.

2. Round Robin

With a finite ready queue and finite quantum/switch costs, RR gives each ready process recurring CPU service.

3. MLFQ with Boosting

Periodically move processes back to the highest-priority queue and reset their allotments.

4. Fair Share Scheduling

Allocate CPU shares to users or groups according to an explicit policy; the allocation depends on runnable demand.
